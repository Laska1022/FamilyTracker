# Capacity and Firebase costs

At a continuous 60-second interval, one device produces up to 1,440 history writes/day; ten produce 14,400. At 300 seconds: 288/device or 2,880/ten. These are request targets, not schedule guarantees. Firestore stores history only, not an extra copy of each live position. Starting/stopping sharing and membership operations add a small number of writes. Retries can produce additional billed writes even though UUIDs avoid duplicate documents.

A fully populated daily route costs up to 1,440 document reads at one minute, or 288 at five minutes, plus a small number of membership/sharing rule lookups and metadata reads. Around 34 full one-minute daily routes already approach the typical Spark 50,000-document-read daily quota; repeatedly browsing all ten people's days can exceed it. Empty queries, rule dependencies and retries also count. Day queries are paginated, not live whole-history subscriptions. Avoid repeated refreshes.

Thirty days for ten devices: 432,000 points at one minute, or 86,400 at five minutes. At an estimated 0.5–2 KB/document including names/index overhead this is approximately 216–864 MB or 43–173 MB. Measure actual Firestore usage: index overhead and data lengths vary and the 1 GiB free storage limit can be reached. The supplied index exemptions avoid indexing coordinates/metadata that are never queried. `deviceTime` retains its automatic single-field index; no composite index is required for a single user's ordered day query.

RTDB sends small live payloads to authorized subscribed peers. For ten continuously connected members, 14,400 updates/day × 9 peers × roughly 0.3–0.7 KB is about 39–91 MB/day (1.2–2.7 GB/month), before connection protocol, initial downloads, membership, retries and encryption overhead. Monitor actual billing/download metrics; this is an estimate. Typical Spark RTDB limits include 1 GB storage and 10 GB monthly downloads. Current location storage is tiny; download fan-out dominates. Map tile traffic is separate.

There is no automatic history TTL or scheduled cleanup. Users can manually delete all their history in the current Circle. Monitor usage in Firebase Console, use five-minute mode where appropriate, and delete older data before quotas are exhausted. Exceeding Spark quotas can interrupt service; it does not justify silently upgrading billing. Recheck published Firebase quotas/pricing for the selected products/region at deployment: https://firebase.google.com/pricing .
